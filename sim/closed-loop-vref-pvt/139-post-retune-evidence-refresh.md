## The tradeoff (stated explicitly, per this issue's AC #2)

This topology (a 2-BJT Brokaw cell) has exactly **one degree of freedom** in
this retune — the `R1/R2` resistor ratio — and that ratio sets **both**
`vref`'s absolute level (via the PTAT term `I*R1` added to `VBE(Q3)`'s CTAT
term) **and** its first-order temperature coefficient (via how well the
PTAT slope cancels `VBE(Q3)`'s own CTAT slope) **simultaneously**. Retuning
`R1` to null the TC (issue #134) necessarily moves `vref`'s absolute level
away from wherever it happened to sit before, and vice versa (issue #133's
alternative direction, re-deriving `R1` to center `vref` at nominal, was
found to leave TC at 457-483 ppm/°C — worse than the untouched-baseline
349-376 ppm/°C, per
[`133-vbe-q3-r1-rederivation.md`](133-vbe-q3-r1-rederivation.md) Sec 3).
**Improving one row with this single degree of freedom necessarily
regresses the other** — this is not a testbench artifact or a sizing bug to
be fixed with more precision; it is inherent to a single-resistor-ratio,
untrimmed Brokaw core. Closing both rows simultaneously needs a second
degree of freedom (a trim network, or an equivalent temperature-compensation
mechanism) — already flagged as explicitly out of scope for this design's
first pass by `design/README.md`'s "Explicitly out of scope" section (issue
#9), and reaffirmed by `measurements/2026-08-tc-retune/README.md` Sec 5
("Side effects and follow-ups"), which documents this exact tradeoff from
the retune's own side.

The Output-reference-vs-TC tradeoff inherent to this topology's single
degree of freedom is stated explicitly here so that wherever the operator's
ratification decision is actually made (PR #128 itself, #125, or a
replacement decision record), the operator is choosing with the regression
in view, not against stale pre-retune numbers.
